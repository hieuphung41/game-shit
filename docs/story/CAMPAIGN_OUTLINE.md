**Chosen story direction:** The Shattered Sovereign Compact. This is a campaign outline; names, scenes and contracts remain to be written.

## Opening: The debt notice

The player returns to a half-seized family manor. An officer of certification presents the final demand: pay the inherited debt within a fixed period or lose the house, the remaining family name and the right to work legally.

An older retriever recognises a contradiction in the debt record. The original ledger was last seen in a flooded customs territory. The player buys a low-grade licence, packs the first kit and enters their first exclusion zone.

**Player objective:** recover enough to survive, then find the original debt record.

## Act I - The Black Tax Port

**Local conflict:** a merchant house has turned a drowned customs port into a toll operation. It hides emergency goods and ledger pages in old warehouses while making independents pay for access.

**Narrative reward:** the player extracts the original debt record. Its seals show the debt was created after the alleged treason, not before it.

**Map logic:** routes around docks, market lanes, rooftops, warehouses and a customs fortress. The player learns extraction, safebox protection, compact versus bulky loot and optional vault risk.

**New lead:** an erased maker’s mark points to an abandoned military forge.

## Act II - The Silent Forge

**Local conflict:** a military house holds the forge and rationing of weapons. Miners and retrievers are disposable labour; the house wants a regulator that can control the region’s failing ward engines.

**Narrative reward:** a recovered forge registry proves the player’s ancestors were assigned to inspect, not sabotage, the old Compact.

**Map logic:** mine works, rail lines, lifts, molten workshops and a sealed foundry. Large ore, armour and weapons make backpack shape and value-per-cell decisions central.

**New lead:** the registry refers to testimony stored at a religious archive where history was rewritten.

## Act III - The Monastery of Broken Mirrors

**Local conflict:** a religious order uses mirrors and old records to control what people believe about the empire’s fall. It has protected refugees, but it has also hidden the names of those sacrificed to keep the seals working.

**Narrative reward:** the player learns that the Compact did not merely regulate salvage; it fed a containment mechanism beneath the capital. A close family member or former mentor disappeared while investigating the same truth.

**Map logic:** courtyards, archive vaults, reflection routes and false doors. Relics are small but high value; map knowledge matters while active routes vary at high difficulty.

**New lead:** a fortress-city is burning ash to suppress the effects leaking from below the capital.

## Act IV - The Ash-Rain Citadel

**Local conflict:** the local house performs a cruel warding ritual to prevent its population from mutating. It is both protector and oppressor. The player can take contracts that reveal incompatible needs: food, medicine, ward fuel and proof of abuse.

**Narrative reward:** confirmation that the Office of Certification created the debt network to identify and control anyone holding evidence against the Compact.

**Map logic:** siege lanes, rain-soaked battlements, evacuation tunnels and ritual engines. The player sees that map states are produced by political choices as well as natural hazards.

**New lead:** a recovered seal opens the final route to the capital.

## Act V - The Unthroned Capital

**Local conflict:** the surviving houses race to either seize, destroy or restore the system below the capital. The person the player has been seeking is alive and has made an opposing choice about what should be done.

**Revelation:** the player’s house helped make the Compact and later tried to expose it. It was neither innocent nor solely guilty.

**Final decision - keep scope controlled:**

1. Restore the Compact with stricter oversight: safety first, but some old power remains.
2. Break the Compact: end monopoly and debt control, accepting a dangerous transition.
3. Claim the mechanism: secure the base and player house, but risk repeating the cycle.

These choices should alter the epilogue, hub state and limited endgame contracts. They should not require three separate full campaigns.

## Story-to-map rule

Before a new map is created, its one-page brief must define:

1. Which faction or historic decision caused this place to become what it is.
2. What immediate personal or material goal sends the player there.
3. What proof, key, resource or relationship comes back on successful extraction.
4. Which gameplay pressure is introduced: capacity, route uncertainty, ranged danger, environmental hazard, etc.
5. Which consequence visibly changes the base or opens the next territory.
