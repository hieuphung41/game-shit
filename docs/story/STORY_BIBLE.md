# Story Bible - Working Draft

**Status:** narrative direction approved on 2026-10-29. Names, scenes and individual contracts remain working draft. [MAIN_STORY.md](MAIN_STORY.md) decides canon for world laws, evidence, Acts, endings and post-campaign consequences; if this working draft conflicts with it, the main story wins.

## 1. Game premise and design constraint

The game is an offline-first, 2.5D action-extraction adventure. A run has a real cost: the player pays for access, chooses a loadout, enters a dangerous zone, loots, decides whether to press further, and must extract to keep the haul.

The narrative must make these rules feel inevitable rather than invented for the game system:

- a player must have a practical reason to enter dangerous places repeatedly;
- money, permits and gear must matter from the first hour;
- death can save the character but lose unsecured property;
- a small safebox can secure only selected objects;
- new regions must open because of story progress, not merely character level;
- maps at higher difficulty may change state while their geography remains learnable.

## 2. Narrative principles

- Start with a personal, material stake: debt, a home, a name, a missing person or a community. Do not begin with “save the world”.
- Treat resources as power. Relics, deeds, weapons, maps and materials must have economic, political or personal meaning.
- No faction is completely right. Every faction needs one defensible aim, one asset it controls and one wrongdoing it hides.
- Every region must be the consequence of historical choices. Architecture, enemies, loot and its boss should say who ruled there and what went wrong.
- Reveal the central mystery in useful pieces. A discovery must change the next concrete objective, not only add lore.
- Use environment, item descriptions and short NPC scenes for optional lore; give a direct objective before every expedition.

## 3. Chosen direction: The Shattered Sovereign Compact

### Premise

An old empire maintained peace through a Compact: great houses administered sealed territories and delivered recovered legacy goods to the throne. When the last sovereign died, the houses divided the Compact, monopolised access and exploited the territories. The seals weakened; each region became dangerous, distorted and valuable.

The player belongs to a border house accused of treason and crushed by an impossible certified debt. To retain their home, name and freedom, they become a licensed retriever: someone who enters restricted territories to recover goods, proof and lost inheritance.

The early goal is simple: earn enough to survive and find the original debt ledger. The later goal is to discover why the debt was created and what the Compact was actually built to contain.

### System-to-fiction mapping

| Game system | Fictional rule |
|---|---|
| Contract entry fee | Licence, checkpoint tax, security bond or bribe paid to the territory holder |
| Prepared loadout | A licensed retrieval expedition from the player base |
| Death loses carried gear and loot | A recall sigil returns its bearer only; unsealed property remains in the exclusion zone and becomes abandoned salvage |
| Safebox | A small certified coffer with limited seal slots; only sealed objects travel with a recalled owner |
| Rarity: white to red | Appraisal classes denoting market value, danger, provenance and legal importance |
| Backpack grid | Physical salvage has real dimensions; compact proof, gems and keys compete with bulky armour, weapons and machinery |
| Hard / extreme map variants | Controlling houses alter patrols and gates; old wards, weather and rituals change the active state of a familiar territory |
| New map access | Only defined evidence, permits or story conditions open the next territory. Faction authority counts only when it is an explicit defined permit or condition, never relationship or reputation alone. |
| Base progression | A seized manor or abandoned retrievers’ guild is reclaimed room by room: warehouse, forge, archive, infirmary and market |

### Central mystery

The player’s house was framed for a present-day act of sabotage, but it is not innocent. Its founders helped build the portable obligation ledger that made the Compact workable, benefited from its early extraction rights, and later tried to expose how the Office of Certification had turned it into a debt-and-forced-labour machine. The certified debt was manufactured to silence the remaining house before it could recover proof.

## 4. Factions - first draft

| Faction | Public claim | Asset | Hidden cost / wrongdoing |
|---|---|---|---|
| Office of Certification | Keeps expeditions legal and casualties controlled | Licences, safebox seals, debt records | Uses debt to strip small houses of access rights |
| The Seal Houses | Protect the territories assigned to them | Gates, troops, regional resources | Hoard legacy goods and worsen the failing seals |
| Retrievers’ League | Protects independent explorers and refugees | Field knowledge, black-market routes | Some members steal sealed goods and sell information |
| Old Wardens — `world-facing/proposed at launch` | Wants every territory closed forever | Knowledge of wards and monsters | Accepts famine and exile as the price of safety. No launch map, actor, contract or campaign gate is authorized without a separate Task Card. |
| The Player’s House | Wants its name and home restored | A disputed hereditary claim | Its ancestors helped construct part of the destructive system |

## 5. Alternative premises retained only as reference

### Ledger of Forbidden Zones

The player inherits a fraudulent debt and works as a recovery agent in legally sealed zones. This keeps the strongest practical motive and may be used as the lower-fantasy version of the leading candidate.

### Estate of the Nameless House

The player is the last field steward of a disgraced house, reclaiming lost holdings. It gives especially strong base-building emotion but needs a wider mid-game conflict.

### Shards of the Boundary Seal

Ancient boundary seals isolate resources and monsters. Factions mine the zones even though doing so risks releasing what they contain. This is the highest-fantasy option and gives broad freedom for map rules.

## 6. Content and tone guardrails

- Tone: serious adventure, political pressure and melancholy ruins; moments of human warmth at the base.
- The player is capable but not a chosen saviour at the start.
- Avoid copying characters, names, terminology, plots, scenes or locations from any reference work. References are structural inspiration only.
- Do not lock the player out of recovery after repeated deaths: basic work contracts and starter loadouts must remain available.
